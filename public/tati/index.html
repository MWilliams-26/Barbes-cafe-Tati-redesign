Pourquoi Tati?

Why is it called Tati?

TITA

Two letters swapped: TITA

Paris, 1948

On Boulevard de Rochechouart in Barbès, a young saddler from La Goulette, Tunisia, named Jules Ouaki opened a 50 m² shop. He wanted to call it Tita, his mother Esther’s nickname. The name was already taken, so he swapped two letters.

Tati grew to fill the boulevard, wrapped in pink-and-white gingham, and for seventy years it was where all of Barbès came to shop. It even took over the old Dupont-Barbès brasserie.

Hoboken, 2024

When the family behind Barbès opened a café on the corner of 4th and Bloomfield, they named it for the shop that belonged to that neighbourhood. Barbès has its Tati again: the big sister uptown, the little sister downtown.

“From Paris to Hoboken: Tati symbolizes the togetherness that kept us strong.”
Café Tati

And the bears? They sit on the awning and at the door, rain or shine. In the café’s words, “our bears tell a story of togetherness and resilience.”

Which house?

Le soir, chez Barbès

When we light the candles, our big sister turns up the music.

Barbès is where Tati comes from: a Paris bistro with a Moroccan heart on the corner of 13th and Park. Pastilla and lamb tagine, steak au poivre, the Rock’n Moroccan at the bar, and live jazz, R&B and soul every Friday and Saturday night.

Barbès is open every evening from 4 pm, and from 11 am for weekend brunch.
