The name

Why the café is called Tati

TITA

Tita was a mother’s nickname. The name was taken, so her son swapped two letters.

The word reads Tita

Paris, 1948

On the Boulevard de Rochechouart in Barbès, a young saddler from La Goulette, Tunisia, named Jules Ouaki opened a small shop. He wanted to call it Tita, after his mother Esther. The name was already registered, so he swapped two letters.

Tati grew to fill the boulevard, and for seventy years it was where all of Barbès came to shop. It even took over the site of the old Dupont-Barbès brasserie.

Hoboken, 2024

When the family behind Barbès opened a café on the corner of 4th and Bloomfield, they named it for the shop that belonged to that neighbourhood. Barbès has its Tati again: one uptown, one downtown.

“From Paris to Hoboken: Tati symbolizes the togetherness that kept us strong.”
Café Tati

And the bears? They sit above the door, rain or shine. In the café’s words, “our bears tell a story of togetherness and resilience.”

Which house?

Our sister restaurant

Evenings belong to Barbès.

Barbès is where Tati comes from: a Paris bistro with a Moroccan heart on the corner of 13th and Park. Pastilla and lamb tagine, steak au poivre, cocktails at the zinc bar, and live jazz, R&B and soul every Friday and Saturday night.

Barbès is open every evening from 4 pm, and from 11 am for weekend brunch.
