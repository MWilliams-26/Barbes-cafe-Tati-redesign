Our story

Barbès is a neighbourhood before it is a restaurant.

In Paris

At the foot of Sacré-Cœur, where the métro runs overhead on iron trestles, Barbès is known as a little piece of North Africa: Paris and the Maghreb sharing the same cafés, markets, music and tables.

Our name pays tribute to that melting pot: a menu that takes its lead from Morocco, balanced with the brasserie classics of a Paris bistro.

In Hoboken

Omar, from France, and Abdellah, from Morocco, met working in New York restaurants and opened the first Barbès in Murray Hill. When that building was sold, they brought Barbès home to Hoboken, where they lived.

Since 2018 it has been on the corner of 13th and Park: exposed brick, a zinc bar, banquettes, and French film stars in fezzes looking down from the walls.

“Merci de votre visite!” Abdellah & Omar

In Paris, Barbès had its own landmark: Tati, the pink-gingham bazaar on the boulevard. In 2024 we opened a café downtown and gave it the name. Read Tati’s story

Le matin, chez Tati

Our little sister starts the day.

Barbès opens in the afternoon. Downtown on 4th & Bloomfield, Café Tati is already pouring espresso at 7: warm croissants, crêpes, shakshuka, and a baguette named after us (chicken, roasted tomato, harissa aioli). At night it turns into a candlelit, BYOB French-Moroccan bistro.
