Barbès & Tati See the new sites

Prepared for Lisa & Omar Balouma and Abdellah Ksiyer · November 7, 2026

Two great rooms. Two websites that have never met.

Barbès has a 4.7 on Resy from about 3,900 reviews and a 4.4 on Google from 778. Café Tati has a 4.5 on Google and was where Emily Jabbour sat down with Patch days before becoming mayor. Online, though, neither one mentions the other, and both are quietly losing customers to dead links, mixed-up hours and delivery apps. Here is what we found, what the new sites do about it, and how the two can sell each other.

Fix these first

  1. Barbèsbarbesrestaurant.com is up for renewal on December 6 29 days

    The GoDaddy registration expires on December 6, 2026. It renewed on about the same date last year, so auto-renew is probably on, but it is worth checking that the card on file is current. If it lapses, the website, the email address and every link in every listing stop working at once.

  2. TatiThe “Website” button on Google leads nowhere Now

    Google lists cafetati.com. You own the domain (registered until 2027), but it isn’t connected to anything, so everyone who taps “Website” gets a browser error. The old Sauce ordering site is gone too, while joe.coffee still links to it. Pointing cafetati.com at the new Tati site fixes all of this in one step.

  3. BarbèsThe menu link on Yelp and Instagram opens a “page not found” Now

    Yelp’s “Full menu” button and the Menus button in the Instagram Linktree both go to the old address barbesrestaurant.com/menu/, which has shown a 404 page since the site was rebuilt. So do the old /about, /make-a-reservation and /gift-certificates pages that still turn up in search. The Linktree’s Resy button also carries a date from August 2023.

  4. BarbèsGoogle is told the site lives at a domain nobody owns

    The site’s robots.txt, sitemap and business data all point to barbeshoboken.com. That domain isn’t registered, so search engines are sent to an address that doesn’t exist, and anyone could buy it.

  5. BarbèsResy gives late guests the wrong phone number

    Resy’s “Need to know” text says to call 201.610.0100; the real number is 201.610.1000. The Resy page also still shows the 2020 COVID “support us” banner.

  6. TatiPickup orders go to the apps, at higher prices

    Café Tati’s own Toast ordering page shows “Closed” every day, so Google’s “Order online” offers only Uber Eats and Postmates. Postmates shows a 2.8 rating, and the apps charge more: a croissant is $5.50 there against $4.50 on Toast, an espresso $5 against $4. Turning Toast ordering on keeps that margin and those customers.

  7. BothHours disagree from one listing to the next

    We found five different sets of hours for Tati across nine sources (7–8, 7–8:30, 7–9, until 10, and “Closed” on Toast). Tripadvisor tells Barbès guests you are open 5 am to 1 am, and a county tourism listing still shows the 2020 hours.

What guests can’t find today

  • Your stories. When the Barbès site was rebuilt, the About page went with it: nothing explains the Paris neighbourhood, or Omar and Abdellah’s road from Murray Hill to Hoboken. Tati’s name story lives only in an Instagram reel.
  • The reasons to come on a given night. Happy hour, Moules Monday and live music with Phil & The Cats appear only in image flyers and on Instagram, which Google can’t read. One Yelp reviewer: “apparently they offered a happy hour but had no idea what it was.”
  • Tati after dark. Dinner and BYOB are Tati’s best-kept secret. It is missing from Hoboken Girl’s BYOB guide and from Hoboken Now’s BYOB list, and most listings describe it as a coffee shop.
  • Each other. Neither website, Linktree or Instagram bio links to the sister restaurant. Guests already connect them, just in the wrong place: Café Tati coffee reviews are turning up on Barbès’s Yelp page.

How the refresh makes them family

The family resemblance is already there. Barbès’s own logo is a red-and-white gingham stamp. Tati is named for the Paris store famous for pink-and-white gingham. Both rooms hang portraits of figures in fezzes. The new sites share one simple, easy-to-read typeface pairing (Instrument Serif for headings, Instrument Sans for everything else), the scalloped awning edge, the arched windows and the family footer, while each keeps its own colours and mood.

Barbès · le soir

  • Moody and earthy: espresso, bordeaux, terracotta clay, olive and candlelit brass
  • A horseshoe-arch window and the scalloped edge of the real awnings
  • One menu, two cities: Côté Paris and Côté Marrakech
  • The week in text: Moules Monday, happy hour, live music, brunch

Café Tati · le matin

  • A polished Paris café: ivory, bistro green, blush and champagne gold
  • Built for coffee, a bite and good conversation
  • The name story: TITA becomes TATI
  • Morning, lunch and BYOB dinner on a printed café card
  • The bears, dogs, babyccinos, the terrace on the park
  • Live hand-offs. Each site recommends the other at the right moment. At 11 am, Barbès’s site says Tati is pouring until 9. At 3 pm, Tati’s site says Barbès opens at 4, with live music on Fridays and Saturdays. Late at night, Barbès points to tomorrow’s 7 am croissants.
  • One day, one family. A shared clock shows today’s hours for both houses: 7 am at Tati to midnight at Barbès.
  • One party planner. Daytime showers and kids’ birthdays are routed to Tati; evenings and bigger groups to Barbès. Both go to one inbox.
  • A shared footer. The family line (Café Tati, La Bohème, Pronto Pasta, Barbès) shows whether the sister is open right now.

Cross-promotion playbook

Proposals for you to decide on; nothing here is live.

  1. Le Carnet. One stamp card for both houses, styled like a book of métro tickets. Coffee at Tati and dinner at Barbès fill the same card.
  2. Le lendemain. A card with the Barbès check: “Breakfast is on 4th & Bloomfield. Show this for a croissant with your coffee.”
  3. Swap the specials. Tati viennoiseries on the Barbès brunch and party menus; a monthly “Barbès night” at Tati with harira and the lamb tagine.
  4. Instagram and Linktree. Each bio names the other; Barbès’s Linktree adds Café Tati (and fixes its Menus link); a shared “From Paris to Hoboken” reel series.
  5. Table cards. A QR card at the Tati counter for Barbès’s live-music nights, and one at the Barbès host stand for Tati’s weekend mornings.
  6. One gift card. Both restaurants already sell Toast gift cards; ask Toast whether one card can work at both.
  7. The press hook. The Barbès-and-Tati name story is a ready-made feature for Hoboken Girl, and a reason to ask them to add Tati to the BYOB guide.

Before anything goes live

  • Hours for both houses, including when Tati’s dinner menu starts and when Barbès brunch ends.
  • Happy hour days and offer (Instagram says Sunday–Thursday 5–7 pm; Google says Thursday 4–7 pm), and whether live music runs on Thursdays too.
  • Tati’s current dinner menu and prices. Ours come from a guest’s photo of the printed menu.
  • Permission for the photos, and the original files. Barbès’s come from its website. Tati’s come from Google listing photos (the uploader isn’t confirmed) and small Instagram previews, so we need originals or a short shoot.
  • Logo files: a vector Barbès stamp and Tati’s round badge.
  • The founding details: Barbès’s Hoboken opening year (we say 2018), and the wording of the Tati name story and the bears.
  • Whether to include La Bohème and Pronto Pasta in the family footer.
  • Party capacity and packages, and which inbox should receive party inquiries (the concept uses info@barbesrestaurant.com).
  • Whether to promote halal chicken and lamb, which Omar confirmed on Yelp some years ago.

In production, each house would live at its own address, barbesrestaurant.com and cafetati.com, with the shared pieces built once. This preview puts both under one link for review. Research, sources and the asset record are in the project’s RESEARCH.md.
